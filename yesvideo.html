YesVideo: From startup to industry leader

[YesVideo Company Overview Image Placeholder]

YesVideo was founded in 1999 by Darren Crystal, Jim Wickett, and myself. We started as a three-person team with a simple but powerful vision: make it easy for consumers to access and enjoy their precious memories stored on aging analog formats like VHS tapes, film reels, and photographs.

The Challenge

In the early 2000s, millions of families had decades of memories trapped on deteriorating analog media. Converting these to digital formats was expensive, time-consuming, and technically challenging for most consumers. Professional services existed but were costly and inaccessible to the average family.

[Automated Processing Pipeline Image Placeholder]

Our Solution

We developed an automated digital media authoring platform that could process thousands of hours of video content with minimal human intervention. Our technology included:

  • Automated scene detection - Intelligently segmenting content into meaningful chapters
  • Quality enhancement - Improving audio and video quality through digital processing
  • Menu generation - Creating professional-looking DVD menus automatically
  • Batch processing - Handling large volumes efficiently while maintaining quality
[Partnership Network Image Placeholder]

Scale and Impact

By the time I left in 2005, YesVideo had grown from our initial team of 3 to over 100 employees. We had established partnerships with major retailers including Kodak and Fujicolor Services, making our services available at over 30,000 store locations worldwide.

We successfully served over 1 million consumers, helping families preserve and access their most precious memories. In the early 2000s, YesVideo was recognized by Forbes as one of the top 100 most promising companies, validating our vision and execution.

[International Operations Image Placeholder]

Technical Innovation

As VP of Engineering, I led the development of our core technology platform. Key innovations included:

  • Scalable processing architecture - Designed to handle massive volumes while maintaining quality
  • Quality control systems - Automated detection of processing issues and quality assurance
  • Customer workflow management - End-to-end tracking from order to delivery
  • International localization - Supporting multiple markets with different technical standards

Legacy and Learning

YesVideo represented a successful example of taking complex technology and making it accessible to mainstream consumers. The company continued to grow after my departure and eventually became part of CVS Health's digital services portfolio.

The experience taught me valuable lessons about scaling technology, building consumer-focused products, and the importance of partnerships in reaching mass markets. These insights proved invaluable in my subsequent work at Numenta.
